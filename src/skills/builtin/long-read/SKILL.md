name: long-read
description: Reads a long article, report or research paper closely and gives its structure, main claims, evidence, method and limits, with where each is in the text. Use when the user asks to understand, summarize in depth or read critically a long text or a paper.
license: Apache-2.0

metadata:

---

1. Read the whole text with read_page; read a very long one in parts and keep track of its sections. Note the authors, the date, where it was published, and for a paper whether it was peer-reviewed or is a preprint.
2. Give, in this order:
   - In two or three sentences: what it says, and why it matters.
   - The structure: its sections, and what each does.
   - The main claims, each with the evidence the text gives (data, experiments, sources) and where it is (section, figure, table).
   - For research: the method, the data and how much of it, and how the result was measured.
   - The limits: what the authors admit, and what you notice yourself (a small sample, no control group, correlation taken for cause, a conflict of interest).
   - Terms the user may not know, briefly.
3. Keep the text's figures and wording exact, and quote short passages for the key points. Keep what the text says apart from your own assessment.
4. When it helps, offer next steps: related work, or questions to ask about it.
